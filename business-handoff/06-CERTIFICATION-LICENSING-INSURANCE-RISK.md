# 06 — Certification, Licensing, Insurance & Risk

**Operator facts:** No certification or license. Based in **Texas**. Wants to train **online and in commercial gyms**. Choosing to **operate without a certification for now**. **Side hustle**, aiming to scale up modestly. Payments via **cash / Zelle** (no card processor planned).

> **This is practical guidance, not legal or tax advice.** Verify specifics with a Texas attorney/CPA and with each gym's policy.

## 1. Is it legal to train clients in Texas without a certification?
- **Yes, generally.** Texas does **not** license or regulate personal trainers — there is no state cert/license required to work as an independent trainer.
- That only covers *government* licensing. **Private gatekeepers (gyms, insurers) are stricter** and are where the real constraints come from.

## 2. The real consequences of having no certification
These aren't legal bans — they're practical gates:
- **Liability insurance:** Most personal-trainer liability policies **require an accredited certification.** Without one you most likely **cannot get insured** → you'd be coaching **uninsured**, personally exposed if a client is hurt.
- **Commercial gyms:** Nearly all require trainers to be **certified *and* insured** to coach paying clients on the premises (employed or independent). Coaching paying clients on a gym floor without that usually **violates gym policy and can get you banned.** → **Your commercial-gym plan is the part most blocked by having no cert.**
- **Credibility & pricing:** No cert weakens trust and caps what you can charge versus certified competitors.

## 3. What this means for WHERE you can sell (key takeaway)
- **Online / remote programming = your viable, lowest-risk lane right now.** No physical presence, no gym gatekeeper, and it fits the app's remote-programming workflow. **Make this the front door** of the business while uncertified.
- **Commercial gyms = realistically need a cert + insurance first.** Options: (a) start online; (b) get an entry-level online cert (ISSA/NASM/ACE — affordable, weeks-to-months) to unlock gyms *and* insurance; or (c) operate against gym policy uninsured — **not recommended.**
- **In-person in your own space or a client's home:** legal in TX without a cert, but **uninsured = high personal liability.** Proceed cautiously, with the protections below.

## 4. Liability exposure & how to reduce it (while uninsured)
You collect medical/PAR-Q data and prescribe exercise — that's real exposure. Reduce it:
- **Attorney-reviewed waiver / assumption-of-risk.** The app already captures a versioned waiver — have a Texas attorney review the wording. Texas enforces well-drafted waivers, **but you cannot waive gross negligence or recklessness.**
- **Require medical clearance** for anyone flagged by the PAR-Q before training. Treat your intake screening as a real gate.
- **Form an LLC** for asset protection. As a sole proprietor (the default) your **personal assets are exposed**; an LLC adds a shield — *more* important because you're uninsured. Cheap to form in TX; confirm with an attorney/CPA.
- **Stay in scope:** coach exercise; **don't give medical advice or medical nutrition therapy.** Keep nutrition guidance general (Texas regulates dietitians; "prescribing" meal plans to treat conditions can cross a line). Keep the app's nutrition notes general.
- **Screen out high-risk clients** (recent surgery, serious cardiac/clinical conditions) until certified + insured.
- **Document everything** (waivers, clearances, programs) — the app helps here.

## 5. Payments & taxes (cash / Zelle)
- Cash and Zelle are fine to start. **All of it is taxable income and must be reported**, even though Zelle/cash usually generate no tax form. Keep your own records from day one.
- Use a **separate account** for the business; track income and expenses.
- As you scale, pair the LLC with a separate account and a quick **CPA** conversation. *Not tax advice — confirm with a CPA.*

## 6. The single highest-leverage move
Even though you're operating without one now: **one accredited certification removes most of the above at once** — it unlocks insurance, opens commercial gyms, and lifts credibility/pricing. It's the cheapest way to expand what you can sell. If you stay uncertified, **lean hard into online** plus the protections in §4.

## 7. What the strategy chat should design around
- **Lead with online/remote offers** — viable now, lowest risk.
- **Do not build the plan on gym-floor training** unless/until certified + insured.
- **Bake in** an attorney-reviewed waiver, PAR-Q gating, scope discipline, and ideally an LLC.
- Put **"get one accredited cert"** on the near-term roadmap as the unlock for in-person/gym growth.
